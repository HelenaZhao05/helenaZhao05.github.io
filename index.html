About

I am an undergraduate student in Computer Science at the University of Toronto, with a minor in Statistics. I am interested in how language models are trained, evaluated, and aligned, especially how the reliability of training signals affects model behavior.

My research experience spans LLM reward reliability, multilingual value alignment, and natural language understanding. Alongside research, I work on practical machine learning workflows as an AI Engineer Co-op at Hard Rock Digital.

My current interests include:

  • LLM evaluation and LLM-as-judge reliability
  • Reward modeling and reinforcement learning for LLMs
  • Language model alignment and behavior

Research

RAR²: Reliability-Aware Rubrics as Rewards for Reinforcement Learning

Research collaboration · 2026

Investigating the reliability of rubric-based rewards for language model training. My contributions include identifying inconsistent judge scores during reproduction, comparing policy models, setting up GPU experiments, and selecting evaluation benchmarks.

VALIGN: A Corpus-to-Behavior Framework for Predicting Multilingual LLM Values

Research collaboration · 2026

Studying associations between positions expressed in pretraining corpora and multilingual language model responses to value questions. I contributed to corpus processing and controlled BLOOM-model experiments.

CantoNLU: A Benchmark of Cantonese Natural Language Understanding

Accepted at the MRL Workshop at EMNLP 2026

A benchmark spanning seven Cantonese NLP tasks. I trained a 32,000-token Cantonese tokenizer, built a surprisal-based linguistic acceptability evaluation pipeline, and compared four BERT models to study cross-lingual transfer.
